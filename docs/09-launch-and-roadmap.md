# Step 9: Launch & Roadmap

## Phase 0 — Weeks 1–2 (starts now)

- Enroll in accelerated HCA 75-hour training
- Consult a WA elder-care attorney (licensing trigger point + Seattle/Everett
  city business licensing)
- Submit the DOH Home Care Agency license application as complete as
  possible — the long pole (Step 4), start immediately rather than waiting
  on training to finish
- Register the business (WA Business License, EIN, city licenses)
- Start building the app in parallel — development doesn't block on
  licensing, and vice versa

## Phase 1 — Weeks 2–6

- Complete HCA training, background check, pass the certification exam
- DOH review/on-site survey continues in the background (timeline outside
  the founder's control)
- Finish building and test the app end-to-end (form → email → admin triage)
- Secure commercial general liability insurance
- Soft-launch privately — test the real flow without public marketing yet

## Phase 2 — Once licensed (target ~8 weeks, flagged at-risk per Step 4)

- Go live publicly; light local marketing (word of mouth, community/senior
  centers, hospital discharge planners)
- Take the first paying client, deliver care personally, invoice manually

## Phase 3 — Post-launch, once revenue is proven (3–6 months out)

- Revisit hiring: build the caregiver job-application flow (deferred from
  MVP, Step 5)
- Formalize the "same business day" response-time SLA (exact hours/days)
- Consider payment automation once manual invoicing becomes a bottleneck

## Phase 4 — Later, optional

- Medicaid/COPES contracting, if demand justifies the added complexity
- Expand beyond Seattle/Everett
- Revisit live-in flat-rate pricing once there's specific legal guidance on
  WA's "reside" wage law
