# Step 4: Trust, Safety & Compliance

## Agency licensing

Operating as a home care agency requires a **WA Department of Health Home
Care Agency license**. Requirements include:

- Certificate of completion from the In-Home Services Orientation Class
- Proof of commercial general liability insurance (WAC 246-335-320(2)(b))
- Disclosure statements and WA State Patrol background checks for the
  on-site administrator and supervisor of direct care services (dated
  within three months of application) — as solo founder, likely both roles
  at launch
- Process: application → DOH review → on-site survey → license issued.
  WAC 246-335-320(4) allows up to **9 months** before DOH closes out an
  incomplete application; actual time depends heavily on how complete the
  submitted paperwork is.

## Caregiver certification (applies to the founder as well as future hires)

Anyone providing paid personal care as a long-term care worker must become
a certified **Home Care Aide**:

- 75 hours of DSHS-approved training (most people finish in 4–6 weeks;
  accelerated programs compress this to ~2 weeks of online theory + 2 days
  in-person skills lab)
- WA State Patrol + FBI fingerprint background check
- Passing score on the HCA certification exam
- Training must be completed within 120 days of hire; full certification
  within 200 days of hire — extended to **365 days** for anyone hired
  through Dec 31, 2027 under emergency rules following SB 5672

## Insurance & workers' comp

- Commercial general liability insurance required for the DOH license
  application.
- Washington is a **monopolistic workers' comp state** — coverage must be
  purchased through WA L&I directly, mandatory once there is any employee.

## Open legal question — action item before first client

It's unclear from public DOH/DSHS sources exactly when solo, unlicensed
personal care crosses the line into requiring the Home Care Agency license
(e.g., whether serving a single client before any hires already triggers
it). **Action item: consult a WA elder-care/healthcare regulatory attorney**
to confirm this trigger point, and to confirm Seattle/Everett city-level
business licensing requirements on top of state licensing.

## Timeline flag

Target of "first paying client in 2 months" (set 2026-07-16) is realistic
for completing HCA training, but at risk for full DOH agency licensure given
the on-site survey requirement. Treat 2 months as the target for
"training-certified and license application submitted," not necessarily
"fully licensed and serving a client." A real week-by-week plan, including
what can legally happen before full licensure, is built in Step 9
