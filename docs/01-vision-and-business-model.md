# Step 1: Vision & Problem Definition

## Problem statement

Families in the Seattle/Everett area (and surrounding communities) struggle to
find caregivers they actually **trust** for hands-on personal care of their
elderly family members. The gap isn't just availability — it's trust: knowing
the person coming into your parent's home is competent, background-checked,
and will treat them with dignity.

## Service

In-home elder care, covering:

- Meal preparation
- Personal care / activities of daily living (bathing, hygiene, changing/incontinence care)
- Mobility assistance (walking, transfers)
- Companionship (conversation, presence, reducing isolation)

## Geography

Launch area: **Seattle, Everett, and surrounding communities** (King and
Snohomish counties corridor). Bounded by realistic drive time for hands-on,
in-person care — this is not a remote/telehealth service.

## Business model shape

The founder is the agency, not a matchmaker for independent competitors:

- Today: founder is the sole, hands-on caregiver.
- Growth path: as demand grows, the agency hires or contracts additional
  caregivers who work under the business. Clients contract with the agency,
  not directly with an individual caregiver.
- The two user-facing roles on the platform:
  - **Clients / families** — request care services, describe needs, schedule visits.
  - **Caregivers** — apply for work with the agency (not self-listing as independents).

## Regulatory flags (detailed in Step 4: Trust, Safety & Compliance)

- WA **Home Care Aide** certification (75-hour training + background check)
  is required for anyone providing paid personal care.
- If/when the agency hires or contracts other caregivers, WA Department of
  Health **Home Care Agency licensing** likely applies, along with liability
  insurance and bonding.
- These requirements will shape both the MVP (Step 5) and the caregiver
  onboarding flow (Step 6).
