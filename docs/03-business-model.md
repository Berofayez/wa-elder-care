# Step 3: Business Model

## Payment source

**Private pay only for launch.** No Medicaid (COPES / Community First Choice)
or long-term care insurance billing at MVP — those require becoming a
Medicaid-contracted provider, which adds a separate layer of state paperwork,
audits, and billing complexity that isn't worth taking on before the core
business is proven. Revisit post-launch if demand and margins justify it.

## Caregiver employment classification

**W2 employees**, not 1099 contractors. Given the agency model from Step 1 —
the agency controls scheduling, methods, and supervision of care — WA's
worker classification rules treat this as employment, not contracting.
Misclassifying caregivers as 1099 would be a real legal and financial risk
(back taxes, penalties, liability exposure). This also means the agency is
responsible for payroll tax withholding, workers' comp, and unemployment
insurance from day one.

## Rates (starting point, not final — revisit once real clients/caregivers exist)

- **Scheduled hourly visits**: charge clients **~$42/hr**, pay caregivers
  **~$24/hr**. The ~$18/hr gross margin covers employer payroll tax burden
  (~10%), workers' comp, liability insurance, and profit.
- **Round-the-clock coverage**: at MVP, structured as **rotating 8–12 hour
  shifts billed hourly**, not a flat "live-in" day rate. Washington's
  "reside" wage exemption for true live-in caregivers (sleeping on-site,
  reduced pay) is legally contested territory with real litigation history
  against agencies that assumed unpaid sleep time. Deferring a flat live-in
  rate until there's specific legal advice on it — flagged for Step 4.

These numbers came from market research on Seattle/Everett-area rates:
independent/private-hire caregivers run ~$26–29/hr, licensed agencies
cluster at $40–45/hr (WA statewide median ~$42/hr), and 24-hour awake-shift
coverage in Seattle runs ~$650–850/day.

## Payment collection

**Manual at MVP** — invoice families by email, paid via Zelle/check/card
reader. No integrated payment processing (e.g., Stripe Billing) at launch;
that's real engineering work (payment schedules, failed-payment handling,
refunds) that isn't justified at solo-founder, low-volume scale. Revisit once
manual invoicing becomes an operational bottleneck.
