# Step 2: Market & User Segments

## Client side

- **Self-directed elder** — capable of making their own care decisions and
  arranging payment, contacts the agency directly.
- **Family-managed** — an adult child or family member acts as decision-maker
  and payer on behalf of an elder who can't manage this themselves.

Both personas are in scope. The intake flow (Step 6) needs to distinguish
"who is filling out this request" from "who is the care recipient," since
they're often different people.

## Triggers for seeking care

All in scope at this stage — prioritization/narrowing for launch happens in
Step 5 (MVP scope), not here:

- **Urgent** — recent fall or hospital discharge
- **Planned** — gradual decline noticed over time
- **Respite** — family caregiver needs relief/backup
- **Social** — elder or family wants regular companionship/conversation, not
  tied to a medical event

## Care intensity / scheduling models

Both in scope:

- **Scheduled visits** — specific hours, specific days per week
- **Live-in / shift-based** — round-the-clock coverage via rotating shifts

## Caregiver side

- Hiring on soft skills first: patience, communication, willingness to learn.
- The agency is open to hiring people without existing certification and
  supporting them through WA **Home Care Aide** certification, rather than
  only recruiting already-certified aides.
- WA allows a caregiver to work under supervision before certification, but
  requires completion of the 75-hour Home Care Aide certification within
  **200 days of hire**. This means the agency takes on real training and
  supervision responsibility, not just job-posting — detailed further in
  Step 4 (Trust, Safety & Compliance) and Step 6 (caregiver onboarding flow).
